- Sampling replaces an integral E[f(x)] by an average of f over samples drawn from p; the error shrinks like 1 over the square root of N.
- Inverse transform sampling: draw u uniform in (0, 1) and solve F(x) = u. It is exact but needs an inverse CDF you can compute.
- Rejection sampling: choose proposal q and constant M with M q(x) >= p(x). Accept x if u M q(x) < p(x). Acceptance rate is 1/M.
- Importance sampling keeps every sample and weights it by w = p(x)/q(x); a poor q gives a few huge weights and an unreliable estimate.
- A Markov chain has a transition matrix whose rows sum to 1. The stationary distribution satisfies pi P = pi and the chain forgets its start.
- Metropolis: propose x', accept with probability min(1, p(x')/p(x)), otherwise stay. p is needed only up to a constant.
- Proposal width matters: too small moves slowly, too large rejects too often. Discard an initial burn-in period.
- Gibbs sampling updates one variable at a time from its conditional distribution; every proposal is accepted.
