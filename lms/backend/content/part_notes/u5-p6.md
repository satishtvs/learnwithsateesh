- Tracking is state estimation: the true state (such as position) is hidden and we only get noisy measurements at each time step.
- The Kalman filter alternates predict (the belief moves forward and uncertainty grows) and update (a measurement is blended in and uncertainty shrinks).
- Kalman gain K = P / (P + R): a large gain trusts the measurement more, a small gain trusts the prediction more.
- Worked step: P = 10.5 and R = 4 give K = 0.7241; the gain and the variance fall over the next steps.
- The Kalman estimate (RMSE 1.13) beats the raw noisy measurements (RMSE 1.96) in the example.
- The particle filter represents the belief by many particles and repeats sample, weight, estimate, resample.
- Particle filters work for any model, not only linear and Gaussian, but too few particles (N = 20) hurt accuracy.
- Exam takeaway: Kalman is fast and exact for linear Gaussian models; particle filters are flexible but costly.
