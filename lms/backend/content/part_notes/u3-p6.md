- Unsupervised learning has only inputs and no labels; clustering groups similar points together.
- k-means minimises `J` (SSE), the sum of squared distances from each point to the centre of its cluster.
- Algorithm (Lloyd): pick K centres, assign each point to the nearest centre, move each centre to the mean, repeat until nothing changes.
- SSE never increases, so k-means always stops, but only at a local minimum.
- In the worked example, SSE fell 228.00, 136.75, 72.84, 11.00 over four passes.
- Choose K with the elbow method (look for the bend in SSE against K) or the silhouette score (higher is better).
- Bad starting centres can trap k-means; use several restarts or k-means++, which spreads the initial centres out.
- Limits: needs K in advance, assumes round clusters, sensitive to outliers. A GMM is the soft-assignment version.
