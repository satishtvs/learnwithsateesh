- Factor analysis assumes a few hidden factors plus per-feature noise produce the observed data: `x = mean + L f + noise`.
- The weights in `L` are called loadings; factors have mean zero and variance one, and the noise is independent for each feature.
- Covariance of x is `L L^T + diagonal(psi)`: the shared part explains covariance, the diagonal holds noise unique to each feature.
- PCA explains total variance with no noise model, while factor analysis separates shared variance from noise and allows rotation (varimax).
- ICA models mixtures as `x = A s` with independent sources, and finds `W` (the inverse of `A`) so that `s = W x`.
- This is the cocktail party problem, also called blind source separation: recover the voices from the microphone recordings only.
- Uncorrelated is not the same as independent: PCA with whitening still leaves a rotation that only non-Gaussianity can find.
- At most one source may be Gaussian; FastICA centres, whitens, then iterates to maximise non-Gaussianity. Order, sign and scale stay unknown.
