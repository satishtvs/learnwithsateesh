- PCA is unsupervised: it rotates the axes so the first axis has the largest variance, the next is at right angles, and so on.
- Centre the data, then the covariance matrix is `C = X^T X / (n - 1)`; its diagonal holds the feature variances.
- Eigenvectors of `C` are the principal components; the eigenvalue is the variance along that direction.
- The sum of the eigenvalues equals the trace of `C`, which is the total variance of the data.
- Five steps: centre (and standardise if units differ), covariance, eigen decomposition, keep top k as `W`, scores `Z = X W`.
- Worked example: eigenvalues 10.9522 and 0.5478, so PC1 holds 10.9522 / 11.5 = 0.9524 of the variance.
- Choose k by explained variance (90 to 95 percent) or the elbow of the scree plot; always standardise when features use different units.
- PCA ignores labels and can mix classes; LDA uses labels and can separate them far better for classification.
