- k-NN stores the training data (lazy, instance-based learning) and does all the work when a new point arrives.
- Steps: compute distances to all training points, keep the k smallest, then vote (classification) or average (regression).
- The answer can change with k: in the worked example k = 1 gives A, but k = 3 and k = 5 give B.
- Distances: Euclidean (p = 2), Manhattan (p = 1), Chebyshev (largest single difference), all from the Minkowski family.
- Always scale features first, using training-data mean and standard deviation, or large-valued features dominate the distance.
- Small k gives a jagged boundary with high variance; large k is too smooth with high bias. Choose k by cross-validation.
- Curse of dimensionality: in high dimensions nearest and farthest points are almost equally far, so neighbours lose meaning.
- Strengths: no training, curved boundaries. Weaknesses: slow prediction, large memory, needs scaling.
