- A hypothesis is consistent if it classifies every training example correctly.
- The version space is the set of all hypotheses consistent with the training data; it shrinks as examples arrive.
- List-Then-Eliminate lists every hypothesis and deletes the wrong ones; correct but impractical for large spaces.
- S holds the most specific consistent hypotheses and G the most general ones; all others lie between them.
- Candidate elimination starts with S all empty and G all question marks.
- A positive example generalises S and removes members of G that reject it; a negative one specialises G and removes members of S that cover it.
- EnjoySport trace: S ends at <Sunny, Warm, ?, Strong, ?, ?> and G at Sunny with ?s and Warm with ?s, so the version space has exactly 6 hypotheses.
- When S and G become the same single hypothesis, the concept is learned; a split vote on a new day means the learner is unsure.
- Inductive bias is the assumption that the target is a conjunction of attribute constraints; without it a learner cannot generalise.
