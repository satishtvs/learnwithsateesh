- A full joint over n yes/no variables needs 2^n - 1 numbers (1023 for n = 10); a graph exploits the fact that most variables depend on few others.
- A Bayesian network is a directed acyclic graph (DAG); each node stores a table P(node | parents).
- The joint probability is the product of P(node | parents) over all nodes, for example P(R) P(S | R) P(W | S, R).
- Inference by enumeration: add the joint probabilities of all worlds that match the evidence, then divide. Here P(rain | wet) = 0.3577.
- Explaining away: seeing the sprinkler on makes rain less likely (0.0068), because the sprinkler already explains the wet grass.
- Chain and fork: observing the middle node blocks the path. Collider: observing the middle node opens the path.
- A Markov random field is undirected: a potential psi on each clique, and P = product of psi divided by the normalising constant Z (in the example Z = 32).
- Exam takeaway: Bayesian networks normalise automatically through their tables; MRFs need Z.
