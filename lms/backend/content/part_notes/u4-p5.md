- A genetic algorithm searches by evolving a population of candidate solutions; it needs only a score, not a gradient.
- A chromosome is a string encoding one candidate and each position is a gene; bits are decoded to a number and scaled into the range.
- The fitness function is the only link to the problem: larger is better, it must be cheap, and bad design misleads the search.
- Roulette wheel selection picks a string with probability fitness / total fitness; it works badly when all fitness values are similar.
- Tournament selection picks t random strings and the fittest wins; a larger t means stronger selection pressure.
- Basic loop: initialise, evaluate, select, crossover, mutate, replace, then repeat until a stop rule is met.
- Worked example: total fitness rose from 26 to 41 (mean 4.33 to 6.83) in one generation.
- Premature convergence (all strings equal) can leave a good but not best answer, so a GA gives no guarantee of the optimum.
